# Gold standard

Last verified: 2026-07-01

Our best work, and what each piece gets right. **Not** the full archive — that's
in `articles/`. These are the ones worth imitating.

When drafting, read the entry that matches the format you're writing.

> **A note about the starter kit:** the five files named below are Lighthouse's
> and are not shipped, for the reason in `articles/README.md`. The entries stay
> because the shape is the point - a pointer to one real file, what to copy from
> it, and what not to.

---

## Blog — `articles/why-site-diaries-fail.md`
**Why it works:** opens with a specific failure on a named type of project, not
with a definition. The comparison table in the middle is the thing people
screenshot and share. Ends without a hard sell.
**Copy:** the structure, and the willingness to say plainly what doesn't work.
**Don't copy:** the length. It ran long at 2,100 words and the last section is padding.

## Blog — `articles/rfi-turnaround-benchmarks.md`
**Why it works:** one original number, properly sourced and properly caveated.
Every claim traceable. It gets cited by other people, which is the whole point.
**Copy:** how carefully it states the limits of its own data.

## Newsletter — `articles/2026-05-14-the-signal.md`
**Why it works:** one idea, 400 words, a clear opinion, no round-up. Opens with
something that happened on a Tuesday.
**Copy:** the opinion-first structure and the discipline of a single idea.
**Don't copy:** the jokes. They worked once.

## LinkedIn (Jana) — `articles/linkedin-2026-06-03.md`
**Why it works:** a real story with details that could only come from someone who
was there. Admits something didn't work. No CTA.
**Copy:** the specificity. "The foreman had 40 photos on his phone and no way to
get them off it" beats any general claim about documentation.

## Launch announcement — `articles/atlas-4-launch.md`
**Why it works:** leads with the problem it solves for a specific person, not
with the feature list. Feature list is a table at the bottom where people who
want it can find it.
**Copy:** the order. Problem, then who it's for, then what it does.

---

**How to use these:** read the relevant entry, then read the actual file. Pattern
match on structure and register, never on phrasing. Reusing sentences from these
pieces is worse than writing new ones badly.
